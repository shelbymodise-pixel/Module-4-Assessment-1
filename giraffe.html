Giraffe

Giraffes are the tallest land animals and belong to the family Giraffidae. They are found in Africa, mainly in savannahs and open woodlands. The two main species are the Northern giraffe and the Southern giraffe. Male giraffes can grow up to 5.5 m (18 ft) tall and weigh up to 1,200 kg.

Giraffes are known for their extremely long necks and legs. Their long necks help them reach leaves high up in trees, especially acacia trees. They also have long tongues, which can be up to 45 cm long, used to grasp leaves. Their spotted coat patterns help with camouflage.

Giraffes are herbivores and mainly feed on leaves, flowers, and fruits. They prefer habitats such as savannahs and open forests where food is abundant.
